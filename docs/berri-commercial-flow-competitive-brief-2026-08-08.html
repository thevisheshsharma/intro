Competitive commercial brief · 8 August 2026

Berri should sell access monthly and meter expensive outcomes quietly.

The launch model is a simple recurring subscription, preceded by a personalized preview and followed by a card-required trial. Explorer is limited; paid plans deliberately show no credits or usage meter. Metering belongs later where usage truly varies: bulk enrichment, CRM automation, and APIs.

Scope: Berri versus Clay, Apollo, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Affinity, and Common Room. Public competitor claims are verified from official sources; Berri recommendations are strategic inferences and launch hypotheses.

Decision

Final launch decision: keep the recurring platform fee, but do not launch with credits or a paid-plan usage meter. Let people connect X and see a small personalized preview without starting a trial. Then ask for a card only when they click Start my 14-day trial. Keep rate limits and provider-cost controls internal.

Overview

1 · Connect XNo subscription and no trial clock. Explain read-only access.
2 · Preview valueShow a personal network preview or one bounded Pathfinder result.
3 · Start trialChoose Founder, see exact future charge, and enter a card in Stripe Checkout.
4 · Full accessFourteen-day trial with a visible end date, no usage meter, and a cancellation link.
5 · Convert cleanlyCharge on the disclosed date or move to read-only if canceled or payment fails.

Why preview first

X access is already a trust decision. Asking for payment before demonstrating a warm path compounds friction.

Why require a card later

Berri has real SocialAPI and AI costs. A contextual card gate filters abuse after the user understands the value.

Why fourteen days

It gives a B2B buyer two working weeks. Activation still needs to happen in the first session and first week.

Credits versus monthly pricing

Pure subscription

Good for: product access, saved graph, monitoring, collaboration, and predictable use.

Risk: an “unlimited” promise can destroy margins on expensive provider calls.

Pure credits

Good for: one-off enrichment, exports, API responses, and other visibly transactional work.

Risk: users hesitate before clicking, struggle to predict cost, and compare credits instead of outcomes.

Recommended launch

Customer sees: a monthly plan with no credits or usage meter.

Berri tracks: internal cost units for SocialAPI, AI, sync, and graph expansion.

Launch rule: maximize healthy paid usage while enforcing server-side rate, concurrency, and abuse controls. If usage pricing is introduced later, charge only for recognizable successful outcomes—not cached results, provider failures, duplicates, unchanged refreshes, or empty results.

Do not publicly sell Growth yet: team collaboration remains marketed rather than verified. Likewise, remove “unlimited searches,” verified exports, SSO, and API claims until each capability has an enforceable product and support contract.

Trial checkout

  • CTA: Start my 14-day trial.
  • Default to monthly; make annual an explicit choice.
  • Show “$0 today, then $99 on [date] unless canceled.”
  • Use Stripe Checkout, subscription trial dates, and webhooks as the billing source of truth.
  • Expose “Manage trial” beside the countdown and in Settings → Billing.
  • Cancel at trial end, preserve access until then, and confirm “You will not be charged.”

CRM and API later

Keep this off the launch pricing page. When the capability is real, sell it as a higher-tier workspace or add-on with a minimum platform commitment plus usage per successful outcome:

  • CRM sync and relationship write-back: workspace add-on or Growth/Scale entitlement.
  • Bulk enrichment: price per successful, changed record.
  • API: monthly minimum plus metered successful results, with spend caps.
  • Implementation, mappings, and governance: one-time services fee.

This is consistent with Clay, LinkedIn, Affinity, and Common Room moving CRM/API depth into higher packages rather than letting it define entry-level pricing.

What to measure before changing prices

Use visitor-to-retained-paid conversion as the north-star metric. Also track preview activation, trial checkout completion, first Pathfinder success, cost per activated account, cancellation before charge, first-payment success, refunds, and day-30/day-60 retention. Do not optimize trial-to-paid conversion alone.

What not to overclaim

  • Competitor pricing does not prove Berri customers will accept a particular price.
  • Public prices may exclude taxes, regional differences, promotions, and negotiated contracts.
  • Future usage policies cannot be designed responsibly until Berri records provider cost and successful-outcome frequency using non-PII telemetry.
  • CRM, team collaboration, public API, SSO, and several exports are not yet release-ready Berri capabilities.
